# Player-Level and Lineup-Based Features for Match Outcome Prediction

## What has this project already tried, found, and adopted/rejected regarding player-level and lineup data?

### Takeaway
Phase 8 established the data pipeline (SportMonks lineups + per-player stats, 2004+ team sheets, per-player stats reliable only from 2015/16 onward); Phase 9 built and ablated an "availability" tier (who's playing, 2006+) and a "performance" tier (how well they play, 2016+) against the incumbent's team-form baseline. The availability block delivered a real, statistically-clearing gain (+0.0106 log loss on 2016+, ~13% of all reducible loss), but the mechanism turned out to be squad-quality/experience proxying, not "team news" (injury/absence) as originally hypothesized; the performance (ratings) block added nothing measurable; and the single most valuable discovery was a team-level (not player-level) feature, `club_goals_relative`, found by accident. Three separate attempts to build a better "importance weight" for absent players (season minutes, goal-involvement share) all failed to move the needle, closing that avenue as "not a weighting problem."

### Cited Findings — internal project sources
- SportMonks plan includes `lineups`, `players`, `Predicted Lineups`, `Formations`, per-fixture `sidelined`; does NOT include a standalone `/squads` or `/sidelined` endpoint (403/404) — injury/suspension data is fixture-scoped only, not a queryable "current state." — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_DATA_AVAILABILITY.md]
- Per-player stat types (60 distinct `type_id`s) cover minutes, rating, goals, assists, shots, key passes, tackles, cards, interceptions, clearances, duels won, dribbles, chances created, saves, captain flag, etc. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_DATA_AVAILABILITY.md]
- Lineups (team sheets) exist from 2004; per-player *stats* are thin before 2015/16 (0-8 distinct stat types, ~1.5 rows/player pre-2015) and become dense (37-65 types, ~13-20 rows/player) from 2016 onward — a provider-wide reporting-quality cliff, not a per-league gap. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_DATA_AVAILABILITY.md]
- Summing player-level stats recovers team totals exactly for most stats but is systematically wrong for shots-off-target and has no player-level equivalent at all for 11 of 23 stat IDs the incumbent trains on (possession %, corners, red cards, penalties, pass %, long passes, attacks, dangerous attacks, etc.) — player data is a superset in grain but subset in coverage. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_DATA_AVAILABILITY.md]
- SportMonks predicts a lineup ~48h before kickoff (22 starters, no bench) and confirms it (with bench) close to kickoff; the historical training archive is all *confirmed* lineups, while ~all serving traffic inside 48h will be *predicted* ones — an explicitly flagged, not-yet-measured train/serve distribution gap. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_DATA_AVAILABILITY.md]
- Availability block (who's in the XI, how settled, who's missing) clears the promotion bar: +0.01057 log loss on 2006+ (95% CI [0.00604, 0.01520]) and +0.01204 on 2016+ (CI [0.00642, 0.01778]), via paired bootstrap against a team-form-only baseline retrained on identical rows/splits/hyperparameters. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Performance block (position-group mean ratings/per-90 rates, 46 features, needs the 2016+ cutoff) adds nothing on top of availability: −0.00150 (CI [−0.00536, +0.00234], "within noise"), and even alone (no team form) only beats team form by an untrustworthy +0.00113 (CI spans zero). — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Trimming the performance block to its top-k most-important features (k=1,4,8,18) never clears; breadth of input (only 18 of 74 available stat types used) is ruled out as the cause since more raw types is empirically "the wrong direction." — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Two features carry ~88% of the availability block's gain: `xi_relative_experience` (career appearances relative to a rolling 200-fixture league mean) alone is worth +0.00536 (CI [0.00197, 0.00887]); the other six availability features together add +0.00131 (CI lower bound +0.00004, barely clears). — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Three independent "importance weighting" schemes for absent key players — head-count, season-minutes-weighted, goal/assist-involvement-share-weighted — all land within noise of each other (+0.00003 to +0.00070 difference); minutes-weighting is nearly circular (+0.981 correlated with the head-count it replaces) because key players are *selected* by minutes in the first place; involvement-share weighting has genuinely independent variance (+0.189 correlated with the count) but still adds nothing (+0.0007 across four features). The absent key players in this data average a *lower* rolling rating (6.96) than the fielded XI (7.03) — i.e., most "key player absent" events in the archive are rotation, not genuine unavailability of a star, which is offered as the likely reason weighting-by-importance doesn't help. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- The single largest player-adjacent discovery, found by accident (a leftover bookkeeping column that looked like it doubled the availability block's gain but was actually a date-correlated artifact): `club_goals_relative` — club goals over a 38-match window, normalized by the league's rolling mean — is worth +0.0035 log loss on its own and needs **no lineup/player data at all**, only fixture scores. It is explicitly flagged as "not a player feature," belongs in the team-level feature library, and is "the most promising thing on this list" for the shipped model, because the incumbent's form window (`MAX_WINDOW = 8` matches) is likely too short for scoring-rate signal (vs. the 38-match window used here). — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Splitting the gain by how disrupted the lineup was (settled 0-3 key absences vs. wholesale 10+) shows a *flat* pattern, if anything strongest on **settled** lineups — directly contradicting the original hypothesis (spec's §1) that the block should earn its keep specifically on depleted lineups. Conclusion: "the block works as a squad-quality measurement, not as team news." — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Design decisions: starters-only (no bench-depth features), because a predicted-XI payload has no bench and bench features would reintroduce the train/serve gap; per-90 rates computed over window *totals* not per-match means (avoids a 5-minute cameo goal reading as 18 goals/90); position-group (GK/DEF/MID/FWD) means + counts as the fixed-size aggregation, encoding formation shape implicitly; league-and-position-average imputation for players below `MIN_APPEARANCES = 3` (promoted teams field ~11 such players every August). — [ml/player_stats/player_feature_lib.py, FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Two recorded bugs: (1) `NaN or 0` evaluates to `NaN` in Python, so unrecorded ratings/minutes silently produced 97-99% null feature blocks before an explicit `numeric()` coercion was added; (2) raw "career appearance count" as an experience feature was actually an era proxy (correlated +0.50 with fixture date, since the archive only starts in 2006) and had to be replaced with a league-relative rolling-mean version. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- **`player_sidelined` (injury/suspension) schema exists but is populated per-fixture only and is not yet wired into any feature** — the schema comment explicitly notes it "cannot be reconstructed historically without leaking information," and Phase 9's open items note that "a real injury or suspension list would be a different feature, not another parameterisation" of the current team-sheet-inferred absence features, deferred to Phase 10's poller. — [ml/player_stats/schema.sql, FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]

### Inferences
- This project has already, empirically, ruled out the naive "team news" framing (a specific star being out should visibly hurt the team) at three levels of sophistication (count, minutes-weight, goal-weight) — the bottleneck is *not* better weighting math, it's that the team-sheet-derived "who's missing" signal conflates rotation with genuine unavailability. This directly motivates unexplored external technique #3 below (injury/suspension data as a distinct signal from rotation).
- The project has effectively already answered "does a squad-quality/strength signal help materially beyond team form" — yes, ~13% of reducible loss — but via an experience/quality proxy, not per-player skill ratings. This suggests market-value-based team strength (external idea, not yet tried) could plausibly outperform the home-grown rating-based performance block, since it's a cleaner long-horizon "who is actually good" signal that doesn't depend on ratings only available from 2016.
- The team's own biggest lineup-adjacent lesson (club_goals_relative) is a warning against assuming player/lineup granularity is the right lever at all — a coarser, longer-window team signal beat every player-level feature tried so far by a wide margin. Any new player/lineup technique proposed below should be judged against this bar, not against 1.0 log loss.

### Gaps
- No internal experiment yet exists on real injury/suspension lists (vs. team-sheet-inferred absence), market-value-weighted lineup strength, or bench-vs-starter depth gap — Phase 9 explicitly scoped these out or deferred them to Phase 10.
- The train/serve gap for predicted vs. confirmed lineups is flagged as unmeasured ("cache holds only ~40 fixtures with a predicted XI, far too few to estimate").

## What is "Phase 9" and what's the documented next step?

### Takeaway
Phase 9 is the offline feature-engineering and ablation phase for the player-stats project (following Phase 8's data-availability probe and backfill): it built the `player_form`/`squad_strength`/availability feature libraries, trained a challenger model, and ran a paired-bootstrap comparison against the incumbent to decide whether to promote squad features. Its findings doc ends with six explicit open items for "Phase 10," the most actionable being: build a real injury/suspension list distinct from rotation-inferred absence, drop the underperforming ratings/performance tier, measure the predicted-vs-confirmed-lineup train/serve gap via a poller, and — flagged as the cheapest and most promising item overall — extend the incumbent's team-form window length using `club_goals_relative`'s insight (38-match window vs. current 8-match `MAX_WINDOW`), which requires no lineup data at all.

### Cited Findings
- Phase 9 open item 1 (injury data): "What remains is not a better weight but a better *definition of absent*: the team sheet cannot separate injury from rotation... A real injury or suspension list would be a different feature, not another parameterisation of this one — and Phase 10's poller is what would make collecting one possible." — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Phase 9 open item 2: "The performance tier should not be carried forward as-is. It costs the 2016 cutoff, halves the training set, and buys nothing." — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Phase 9 open item 3: "The train/serve gap is still unmeasured... Phase 10's poller should accumulate predicted XIs alongside the confirmed ones that follow, which turns this into a directly measurable quantity." — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Phase 9 open item 5 (flagged "cheapest promising experiment... most likely to help the shipped model"): extend team-form window length per `club_goals_relative`'s finding that a 38-match normalized goal-volume window beats the incumbent's 8-match window; this is a team-level, not player-level, change and belongs in `ml/feature_lib.py`. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Phase 9 open item 6: positions are missing on ~15% of 2006-2015 starters (~0.03% of 2016+), currently costless since the availability tier is position-agnostic, but would matter for any position-aware feature extended before 2016. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]

### Inferences
- The branch name `player-stats-phase8` alongside a *committed* Phase 9 findings doc suggests the repo's git history/branch naming may lag the actual work done (Phase 9 work is already complete per the findings doc and commit log: `git log` shows commits like "Test goal involvement as the importance measure" and "Measure the squad block against the noise floor" dated after the Phase 8 backfill commits). The active frontier per the findings doc is Phase 10 (a lineup/injury poller), not Phase 8 itself.

### Gaps

## What external, not-yet-tried techniques exist for turning player/lineup data into team-strength/outcome signal?

### Takeaway
The literature supports several techniques this project hasn't tried: (1) market-value-weighted lineup strength (Transfermarkt-style), which academic work shows beats FIFA-ranking-based team strength and generates positive betting returns; (2) combining team-level ratings (Elo) with player-level ratings as *complementary* covariates, shown to significantly beat either alone; (3) genuine injury/suspension data as distinct from rotation (already identified internally as the missing piece); (4) plus-minus-style player ratings aggregated to team level; (5) passing-network/graph cohesion metrics, which the literature says add predictive value on top of standard stats but require event/tracking data this project's provider (SportMonks) doesn't appear to expose at that granularity. Bench-quality-gap and age/rotation-continuity features are discussed in industry writeups but lack the same rigor of quantified academic log-loss results as the market-value and plus-minus lines of work.

### Cited Findings
- **Market-value-weighted lineup/team strength**: Peeters (2018) used Transfermarkt values aggregated to team level to predict match outcomes and found they "perform better than do the FIFA rankings"; a related paper concludes crowd-sourced Transfermarkt valuations can be used to generate betting returns (a strategy of betting when a majority-predicted outcome occurs produced average returns of 1.317%). — [Testing the Wisdom of Crowds in the field: Transfermarkt valuations and international soccer results](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207017300754); [The Wisdom of Crowds and Transfer Market Values (Coates)](https://hcapps.holycross.edu/hcs/RePEc/fek/Session08.2-Coates.pdf)
- Independent applied write-up: "Predicting English Premier League games using Transfermarkt market values" builds match predictions directly off Transfermarkt-derived squad valuations. — [vkontech.com](https://vkontech.com/predicting-english-premier-leagues-games-using-transfermarkt-market-values/)
- **Player ratings as a complement to team ratings**: Arntzen & Hvattum (2021) compare Elo-based team ratings and plus-minus-style individual player ratings as covariates (pre-match Elo difference vs. average difference in starting-XI individual ratings) and find: "Team ratings and player ratings perform about equally well when predicting match outcomes, but forecasts made when using both team ratings and player ratings as covariates are significantly better than those based on only one of the ratings." — [Predicting match outcomes in association football using team ratings and player ratings, J. Quant. Anal. Sports / SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1471082X20929881)
- **Plus-minus player ratings**: A dedicated line of research (Pantuso & Hvattum) develops goal-differential, expected-goals, and expected-points plus-minus ratings for soccer players and aggregates the 15 highest-rated players per team into an average "team strength" measure to compare league/team quality over time. — [Plus-Minus Player Ratings for Soccer (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04943); [ScienceDirect version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377221719309373)
- Related: a "weighted plus/minus metric for individual soccer player performance" (Schultze & Wellbrock, 2018) and "Offensive and Defensive Plus-Minus Player Ratings for Soccer" (MDPI, 2020) extend this into split offense/defense contributions, and a 2024 paper develops "Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus Models... using Possession Sequences" — the modern, data-hungrier end of this family that needs possession-sequence event data. — [content.iospress.com](https://content.iospress.com/articles/journal-of-sports-analytics/jsa225); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/20/7345); [arXiv 2407.17832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17832)
- **Passing-network / team-chemistry graph features**: "On the use of passing network indicators to predict football outcomes" and "We know who wins: graph-oriented approaches of passing networks for predictive football match outcomes" (2025) both report that passing-network-derived graph metrics are predictive of match outcomes, and that "combining passing network approaches with traditional match statistics provides more accurate predictive models than when used separately." One study finds "models based on passing networks being as effective as traditional models using general match statistics," and another notes cohesion/interconnection metrics (e.g., "lower team passing dependency for a given player and high intra-team well-connected passing relations") correlate with better outcomes. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705121002604); [Journal of Big Data (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40537-025-01203-9); [arXiv 2409.13098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13098)
- **Lineup news moving betting markets** (relevant because market data is explicitly out of this researcher's scope, but the mechanism matters for feasibility reasoning): one industry source states a confirmed XI change can shift win probability by "2% to 9%... depending on the player's role and the quality of the replacement," and that a high-volume chance creator's absence "typically moves the Asian handicap line by 0.25 to 0.5 goals and the 1X2 price by 0.15 to 0.40 decimal points," with market movement beginning "within one to three minutes of the official announcement." This is an industry/practitioner source (footyforecast.co.uk), not peer-reviewed, and should be treated as directional rather than a hard number. — [Football Lineup Impact Analyzer](https://footyforecast.co.uk/football-lineup-impact-analyzer/)
- **Squad depth / bench quality as a distinct concept**: industry analytics writeups (Analytics FC, nO THROW, Soccer Wizdom, performanceodds.com) discuss squad depth and bench-player contribution ("bench players... ranked 13th-17th in minutes played") as meaningful for surviving fixture congestion and injuries, but none of the search results surfaced a peer-reviewed, quantified log-loss/accuracy study isolating "starting-XI vs. bench quality gap" as a standalone predictive feature — this appears to be a plausible, discussed-but-not-rigorously-quantified idea in public analytics writing. — [Analytics FC](https://analyticsfc.co.uk/blog/2024/03/13/squad-depth-and-bench-players-contribution-and-strategy/); [nO THROW](https://nothrow.com/squad-depth-in-football/); [Soccer Wizdom](https://soccerwizdom.com/2024/05/06/the-importance-of-squad-depth-in-soccer/)
- **Fixture congestion / rotation fatigue and injury risk**: a Springer Nature systematic review states congested fixture schedules are associated with increased match injury incidence in the short term ("Five studies identified that congested fixture schedules expose players to increased match injury incidence... injury risk overall increased during fixture-congested periods"), though "long-term fixture congestion may have no effect on... injury incidence." This is sports-medicine literature about player health outcomes, not directly about match-outcome prediction, but supports using days-since-last-match / fixture-density as a proxy for squad fatigue risk (which this project already has as `days_since_last_match`). — [The Effects of Fixture Congestion on Injury in Professional Male Soccer: A Systematic Review (Sports Medicine, Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01799-5); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9758680/)

### Inferences
- Market-value-weighted lineup strength is the most rigorously-evidenced not-yet-tried idea in this space: it has a direct academic precedent (Peeters 2018) beating a comparable baseline (FIFA rankings, analogous to this project's rating-based performance block), is computable purely from lineup announcements + a market-value table (no event/tracking data), and targets exactly the gap this project's own performance-tier ablation left open (ratings-from-provider added nothing; a different, external "how good is this player" signal — market value — has not been tried).
- The academic finding that team ratings (Elo/form) and player ratings are *complementary* rather than redundant somewhat cuts against this project's Phase 9 finding that its performance block was "redundant with team form" (R² ~0.3) — worth noting as a point of tension: this project's home-grown per-90-rate-based ratings may simply be a weaker signal than Elo-style player ratings or market values, not evidence that *no* player-level rating can complement team form.
- Passing-network/graph cohesion features are real and evidenced in the literature, but as flagged by the user's own scoping note, they require positional/event tracking data (touch-level or pass-level x/y coordinates), which SportMonks' `lineups`/`player_fixture_stats` includes (aggregated per-fixture counters) almost certainly do not provide — this is very likely infeasible without a different, more expensive data source (e.g., Opta, StatsBomb, or tracking vendors), and should be flagged as such rather than pursued.

### Gaps
- Could not find a rigorous, quantified academic study isolating "starting-XI vs. bench/reserve quality gap" as its own predictor variable (distinct from general squad-depth commentary) — this remains a plausible-but-unverified idea.
- No specific published number found for age/experience-curve features as standalone predictors of match outcome (only general squad-depth/rotation commentary); this project's own `xi_relative_experience` finding (+0.0054 log loss) is the most concrete evidence available on this exact idea, and it's internal, not external literature.
- Could not verify SportMonks' actual event-data granularity (whether pass-by-pass data is available on this plan) from web search alone; this would need to be checked against the project's own SportMonks plan/API docs, which is outside this researcher's scope (internal data probing).

## Which of these techniques are feasible given the project's documented data access (SportMonks lineups + per-fixture player counters, no confirmed event/tracking feed) vs. which need data this project can't get?

### Takeaway
Feasible with current or trivially-extended data access: market-value-weighted lineup strength (needs a Transfermarkt scrape or similar, joined by player_id — a new but modest data source, no event data), real injury/suspension-based lineup deltas (data already exists in SportMonks' per-fixture `sidelined` include per Phase 8's own probe, just not yet wired into features), and squad-depth/bench-vs-starter gap and age/experience features (fully computable from lineups already cached). Infeasible or high-cost: passing-network/graph cohesion (needs event/tracking-level positional or pass data SportMonks' documented endpoints don't appear to expose), and true adjusted plus-minus models (need possession-sequence event data per the arXiv 2407.17832 methodology, not just per-match aggregate counters).

### Cited Findings
- This project's own probe already confirmed the `sidelined` per-fixture include returns real injury/suspension category and reason data ("`sidelined` | OK — 1–7 rows per fixture"), and a `player_sidelined` table already exists in schema but is unpopulated by any writer yet — so the injury-based feature is a data-plumbing task (Phase 10's poller), not a new data-access problem. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_DATA_AVAILABILITY.md, schema.sql]
- The plus-minus/adjusted-plus-minus literature's more advanced variants explicitly require "possession sequences" (arXiv 2407.17832 title: "Regularized Adjusted Plus-Minus Models for Evaluating and Scouting Football (Soccer) Players using Possession Sequences") — a materially more granular data feed than per-fixture aggregate counters. — [arXiv 2407.17832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17832)
- The passing-network papers build their graphs from pass event data with sender/receiver/location, which is a different data class than the per-player aggregate stat types (goals, shots, tackles, minutes) this project's `player_fixture_stats` table stores. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705121002604)

### Inferences
- Given this project's documented preference for measuring rather than pre-selecting (per the user's own stated research style) and the fact that the two "cheap, no-new-data-source" ideas (market value via scrape; injury data via existing `sidelined` include) are also the two best-evidenced in the literature, these should be prioritized for a Phase 10-or-later ablation before any event/tracking-data-dependent idea is considered, which would require sourcing/paying for a different provider entirely.

### Gaps
- Whether Transfermarkt data can be legally/reliably scraped at the volume this project needs (41k+ fixtures, ~19k players) was not investigated here — that's a data-acquisition/ToS question outside search-based research.
- No published source specifies whether SportMonks (this project's actual provider) sells an event/tracking-data tier; this would need to be checked directly against SportMonks' own product plans, not via general web search.

## What is the realistic expected log-loss improvement from lineup-quality features, per literature and vs. this project's own findings?

### Takeaway
Neither this project's own findings nor the literature surfaced report log-loss numbers in isolation for "lineup quality" as a clean, universally-agreed figure, but triangulating: this project measured +0.0106 (from 0.99979 team-form-only down to ~0.98922 with the full squad block) against a denominator where the *entire* reducible loss (versus a base-rate model) is only ~0.084 — i.e., squad/lineup features bought ~13% of everything achievable. The one academic paper found that directly compares team ratings vs. player ratings as covariates reports a *combined* model beats either alone "significantly," but does not appear (from search-result snippets) to quote a specific log-loss delta, and no other study returned quantified log-loss improvement numbers for lineup-quality-only features specifically.

### Cited Findings
- This project's own reducible-loss framing: base rates score 1.07329 log loss; team form alone captures 87.4% of all reducible loss (down to 0.99979); the full squad block (availability + performance) gets the rest of the way to 0.98922, ~100% of currently-captured reducible loss on this dataset/model. The availability block alone is ~90.0% of reducible loss captured, i.e., ~2.6 percentage points of "reducible loss share" beyond team form alone. — [ml/player_stats/FINDINGS_PHASE9.md]
- Arntzen & Hvattum (2021): combining team (Elo) and player ratings "significantly better" than either alone — directional confirmation, but the abstract/snippet available via search did not quote a specific log-loss magnitude. — [SAGE journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1471082X20929881)
- No other source returned a directly comparable, quantified log-loss number for "starting lineup strength" specifically as a predictor (as the assignment's key question hoped to find); the closest is the betting-market-movement figures (2-9% win-probability shift per lineup change) from an industry (non-peer-reviewed) source, which is a probability-shift claim, not a log-loss/accuracy improvement claim, and cannot be directly converted to log-loss without knowing base rates and calibration. — [footyforecast.co.uk](https://footyforecast.co.uk/football-lineup-impact-analyzer/)

### Inferences
- Absent a directly comparable external log-loss benchmark, this project's own +0.0106 (already measured, already adopted) is probably the single best available anchor for "how much lineup/squad-quality features are worth" in a log-loss sense for this exact modeling setup — external literature confirms the *direction and existence* of the effect (player ratings add value beyond team-level ratings) but not a magnitude that would recalibrate expectations up or down.
- Given how small the total reducible loss is (~0.084) for football prediction generally (confirmed by this project's own class-prior benchmark), the realistic expectation for *any* new lineup-derived feature (market value, injury data, bench depth) should be measured in the same units this project already uses — hundredths of a log-loss point, not tenths — and any claim of a much larger gain should be treated with suspicion (as this project's own "accidental" +0.01162-vs-real-+0.00608 date-correlation bug illustrates).

### Gaps
- Could not find a study reporting log-loss (specifically) before/after adding lineup-strength features on a comparable multi-league dataset at comparable scale (this project's ~40k fixtures) — most academic papers report accuracy/RPS or betting-return metrics instead of log loss, making direct magnitude comparison to this project's 0.98922 benchmark difficult.
- Could not verify whether the betting-market lineup-impact figures (2-9% win-probability shift) are peer-reviewed or come from a rigorous backtest; the source (footyforecast.co.uk) appears to be an industry/content site rather than an academic or bookmaker-disclosed source, and should be weighted accordingly (flagged, not treated as fact) in the final report.
