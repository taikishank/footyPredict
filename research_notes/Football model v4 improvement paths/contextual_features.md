# Contextual team-level features (venue, opponent adjustment, league scope, time-window/recency) for v4

## What has this project already tried, and why did it fail or succeed (venue effects)?

### Takeaway
Every venue-related hypothesis tested has been measured null or worse, with one narrow exception: correcting a stale, frozen home-field-advantage (HFA) parameter inside the Poisson rating buys a statistically-insignificant +0.00035 log loss. Club-level "travels well / travels badly" as a trait does not exist in this project's archive — a club's venue gap does not predict its own other half of matches, and the value is dominated by sampling noise.

### Cited Findings
- `FINDINGS_VENUE.md`: a club-level home-advantage split (`VenueAttackDefence`: four ratings per club — attack/defence × home/away) scored −0.00102 vs. the served v3 rating on 19,797 fixtures / 5 rolling folds, and a placebo with the *identical parameter shape* but a random (non-venue) split of evidence scored −0.00242; the real split beat the placebo by only +0.00141 with an interval crossing zero (P(venue better)=0.969) — i.e., statistically indistinguishable from "halving the evidence behind a rating is bad, and venue is not a real reason to do it." — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]
- Reliability check: league-mean venue gap = 0.482 ppg; club-to-club spread within league-season (sd=0.373 ppg) is *smaller* than the spread expected from sampling noise alone (sd=0.431 ppg over ~19 matches/venue) — there is no room left for a real club-level trait. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]
- Split-half reliability of a club's own venue gap: r = −0.0314 (negative), and lag-1 season-to-season persistence in points (r=+0.0359) and goal difference (r=+0.0165) both fall inside the shuffle-null 95% interval — a club's home/away split on half its matches does not predict the other half. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]
- League-wide (not club-level) home advantage genuinely fell across the archive: log(home goals/away goals) went from 0.3029 (2005–2015) to 0.2011 (2021–2025), and to 0.1187 in the 2020 empty-stadium season; the served `AttackDefence.hfa` parameter updates too slowly (`lr * 0.0002` per fixture) to track this and stayed frozen near 0.20–0.27, overstating home advantage throughout the whole validation era. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]
- Raising the HFA learning rate 10x (`hfa_lr10`) corrected the marginal home/draw/away rates almost exactly (model P(home) moved from 0.4512 to 0.4467 against an actual 0.4406) and bought +0.00035 log loss [−0.00025, +0.00097] — a genuine, correctly-signed bias fix that does not clear statistical significance. Raising it 100x, or using a 380-fixture rolling estimate instead of a learning rate at all, both did *worse* than the 10x rate (0.98513 vs 0.98461) — tracking the drift more closely is worse, because a season's HFA estimate carries ~0.03 log-scale noise against only ~0.10 drift over two decades. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]
- Away-win prediction remains the worst-predicted outcome (loss 1.0355) even after the HFA bias fix, and this residual is explicitly attributed to *not identifying which away sides are strong* — i.e. an opponent-strength/rating shape problem, not a venue/calibration problem. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]
- The project's internal rule, stated explicitly after this and the opponent-adjustment phase: "a quantity's spread across the rows the model sees bounds what any feature built on it can be worth, and that bound is cheap to compute" — before fitting anything, check whether the club-level (or opponent-level) signal survives a within-sample reliability/variance-ratio check. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md, ml/FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md]

### Inferences
- The project has ruled out club-specific venue effects (travel/away form as a stable trait) about as thoroughly as a single archive can: two independent statistical tests (variance-decomposition and split-half reliability) both say the signal doesn't exist, not just that it's hard to extract.
- What *is* real and still slightly underexploited is league-level HFA drift, but the effect size is tiny relative to the 0.0211 gap to the market closing line (`FINDINGS_MARKET.md`), so it was correctly deprioritized rather than shipped.
- The unresolved "away win" residual strongly suggests any external venue-adjacent idea (crowd, referee, travel/altitude) is only worth pursuing if it plausibly explains *away-side* performance specifically, not a generic home/away split.

### Gaps
- FINDINGS_VENUE.md doesn't test whether *neutral-venue* fixtures (cup finals, tournament play, COVID-relocated games) are present in the archive at all, or handled as a special case — this project appears to be pure domestic league data (5 leagues, `league_id` 8/82/301/384/564), so neutral-venue handling is likely moot for scope but flagged as a gap.

## What has this project already tried on opponent-strength adjustment, and why did it fail or succeed?

### Takeaway
Opponent adjustment of the 27 raw form statistics (possession, shots, cards, etc., averaged over the trailing 8-match window) was rigorously pre-registered, found to have a real per-match mechanism, but the effect is erased by averaging over a schedule that is *self-correcting* (round-robin leagues mean a club's last 8 opponents are already close to average strength — the observed opponent-strength spread across 8-match windows was actually *below* what a random draw would produce). The idea is closed; goal-based opponent adjustment already exists and is absorbed into `atkdef` (Poisson attack/defence ratings), which is described as strictly more informative than any raw stat correction.

### Cited Findings
- Pre-registered bar (set in advance, mechanism-based): the schedule had to explain ≥5% of variance in ≥5 of 27 form fields (headroom) AND show positive out-of-sample R² gain on a chronological holdout predicting the club's own next-match value (reliability) AND at least one qualifying field had to be outside {goals_for, goals_against} (non-redundancy, since `atkdef` already covers goals). — [ml/FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md]
- Result: bar not cleared — 0 of 27 fields reached 5% schedule-share; the largest was `goals_against` at 3.9%, and the largest non-goal field was `possession` at 1.5%. Per-match, the mechanism is real and large: possession per-match R² on opponent played = 0.0896, goals_against = 0.0749 (better defence measurably suppresses possession/shots/goals and inflates what's conceded) — but averaged over an 8-match window, the opponent term's spread collapses (`opp_atk` sd 0.1994 per-match → 0.0589 over 8; `opp_def` 0.1563 → 0.0458), and this collapsed spread sits *below* a shuffled-opponent null (95% CI [0.0733, 0.0748] for opp_atk) — a club's last 8 opponents are ~80% as varied as 8 drawn at random, because playing the league's strong sides early leaves the weak ones later (round-robin self-correction). — [ml/FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md]
- The correction-to-spread ratio for the best-surviving field (possession): the whole opponent adjustment is 0.9 percentage points against a club-to-club form spread of 7.3 points → only 1.5% of variance moved. Out-of-sample ΔR² on predicting a club's own next-match value (already knowing the *next* opponent, as v3 does) topped out at +0.00164 (possession) and was negative for penalties/tackles/saves/red cards — two to three orders of magnitude below anything that has ever moved this model's log loss. — [ml/FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md]
- The staged, cost-escalating plan (2-column `opp_faced` → per-stat residual adjustment → 27-column per-stat "allowed ratings") was abandoned at stage 1 specifically because the variance-ratio pre-check showed none of the later, more expensive stages could possibly pay for their own parameter count. — [ml/FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md]
- `club_goals_relative` (a 38-match opponent-agnostic scoring-rate feature, once worth +0.0054 as a v1→v2 feature) is now worth 0.00018 [-0.00011, +0.00046] — a Poisson-fitted `atkdef` rating, which is inherently opponent-adjusted and long-horizon with no cutoff, absorbed all its value. — [ml/FINDINGS_WINDOW.md]

### Inferences
- Goal-based opponent adjustment (via the Poisson attack/defence rating) is doing essentially all of the opponent-adjustment work this project can extract from its data; adjusting the *non-goal* match statistics for opponent strength is a dead end specifically because of round-robin schedule self-correction over an 8-match horizon.
- This finding would *not* necessarily generalize to a shorter form window (e.g., 3–4 matches) or to a knockout/cup context where schedule self-correction doesn't apply — but the project's own window sweep (`FINDINGS_WINDOW.md`) found 4-match windows perform *worse* overall, so combining "short window" with "opponent adjustment" was never tested and is a plausible unexplored corner (see Gaps).
- The finding is a caution specifically against *statistical* opponent-strength adjustment (shots, cards, possession-allowed) — it says nothing about *rating-shape* changes to the goal model itself (e.g., Dixon-Coles low-score correlation, which the project is already working on separately per FINDINGS_RATINGS.md, out of scope here).

### Gaps
- Whether opponent adjustment would survive on a *shorter* (3–4 match) form window, where schedule self-correction has had less time to average out the strong/weak split, is untested — the project only tested it at the served 8-match window.
- Whether opponent adjustment matters more for extreme fixtures (e.g., a club that has played 8 consecutive top-4 sides due to fixture pile-up, cup replays, or European commitments) rather than as an average effect, is not tested; the aggregate null could mask a fat-tailed subgroup effect.

## What has this project already tried on league/competition scope, and why did it fail or succeed?

### Takeaway
League identity — as an ID, one-hot dummy, rolling "league character" descriptor, per-league interaction terms, or fully separate per-league models — is worth zero to strongly negative everywhere it was tested; the one positive-looking arm (league × rating interactions) was a tree-depth artifact that a random, football-blind partition reproduced equally well.

### Cited Findings
- Rolling-origin ablation (19,797 fixtures, 5 folds), all arms on top of served v3 (63 cols): `id` (league_id column) = −0.00001 [−0.00016, +0.00013]; `onehot` (5 dummies) = −0.00001 [−0.00015,+0.00015]; `character` (4 rolling descriptors of league play-style/scoring level) = −0.00005 [−0.00040,+0.00030]; `per_league` (5 fully separate models) = −0.00841 [−0.01001,−0.00681], the worst result in the study, losing in every single league from −0.00595 to −0.00985. — [ml/FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md]
- Per-league outcome-rate differences are statistically significant (χ²=25.6, dof=8, p=0.0012) but a per-league class prior scores *worse* out-of-sample than one global prior (1.07308 vs 1.07261, gain −0.00047 [−0.00097,+0.00005]) — because a league's own home-win rate drifts more across time (e.g., League 8: 0.453→0.462→0.412→0.470→0.420 across five multi-year folds, a 5.8pp swing) than leagues differ from each other at any one time (3.5pp cross-sectional spread) — "pooling 26 years is what makes the differences significant; it is not what makes them useful." — [ml/FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md]
- The one positive arm, `interact` (5 league dummies × 3 rating-expectation columns = 20 extra columns), scored +0.00052 [+0.00016,+0.00090] — but a placebo with fixtures randomly (not by league) split into 5 groups, given the identical 20-column shape, scored *better*: +0.00074 [+0.00034,+0.00113], beating `interact` by +0.00021 (though this delta's own CI crosses zero). Diagnosed as `MAX_DEPTH=4` starving the tree of interaction capacity — handing it any 20 pre-multiplied copies of its strongest signal helps regardless of what the multiplier means. — [ml/FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md]
- An xgboost classifier trained on the 63 v3 columns can predict `league_id` at 28.5% accuracy out-of-time (vs 21.2% majority-class baseline, 20% chance) — so league identity is *not* redundant/already-known information, it is genuinely learnable from other features, yet learning it explicitly still buys nothing. — [ml/FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md]
- Per-league raw loss varies a lot (0.9717 to 1.0037, a spread bigger than the entire gap to market), but the market's own per-league closing-line loss shows the *same* ordering and similar spread (0.9468 to 0.9838) — the model closes a consistent 77.6%–81.4% of the market's edge in every league; the spread reflects league difficulty, not a league-specific model deficiency. — [ml/FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md]

### Inferences
- This project's 5 leagues, despite covering ~26 years and showing real within-league temporal drift (home-advantage decline, scoring-rate changes), do not differ from each other by more than temporal drift makes them differ from themselves — so any league-scope feature is fighting the wrong axis of variation. The right axis (time) is covered separately under time-window/recency findings below.
- The `per_league` result (separate models cost −0.0084, an order of magnitude worse than any other arm) is strong evidence against ever partitioning training data by league for this dataset size (~4,000 fixtures/league/fold) — data efficiency dominates any league-specific signal.
- The tree-depth artifact discovered via the `interact`/`placebo` comparison is itself a live, open, *hyperparameter* lead (see below) distinct from league scope.

### Gaps
- FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md flags its own open item: a `MAX_DEPTH` sweep on the rated feature set was never run to check whether the `placebo`-driven interaction gain generalizes to non-football, differently-scaled column products in general (a regularization/hyperparameter question, out of this report's scope but worth flagging to whoever owns hyperparameter tuning for v4).
- No test of promotion/relegation cross-league linking or of treating leagues jointly on a single rating scale (all 5 leagues here appear to be separate, non-linked competitions within `AttackDefence`, each with its own `mu`/`hfa`) — this is a genuinely different technique from "league identity as a feature" and is untested (see external section below).

## What has this project already tried on time-window / recency / decay, and are there partially-explored ideas worth revisiting?

### Takeaway
The trailing-match-count form window was swept exhaustively (4 to 38 matches) and found flat-bottomed between 6–24 with 8 already optimal; a separate phase found and fixed a real train/serve mismatch in window *scope* (cross-season vs. in-season), which bought coverage but not accuracy. Genuine recency-weighting (exponential/soft decay, as opposed to a hard trailing-count cutoff) has *not* been tried, and the league-drift table turned up an explicit, still-open question about recency weighting on training rows.

### Cited Findings
- Window sweep (19,810 fixtures, 5 rolling folds), all arms carrying identical `atkdef` ratings: w4 = −0.00142 [−0.00223,−0.00058] (worse), w6 = −0.00046 (null), **w8 (served) = 0.98521 baseline**, w12 = +0.00042 (null), w16 = +0.00026 (null), w24 = −0.00021 (null), w38 = −0.00106 [−0.00209,−0.00008] (worse). Curve is flat-bottomed 6–24, both tails significantly worse; best arm (`g38_s8`, goals over 38 matches + stats over 8) reaches +0.00040 [+0.00000,+0.00079] — a "best of eleven arms" result whose lower CI bound is exactly zero, explicitly not promoted. — [ml/FINDINGS_WINDOW.md]
- The original motivation for a longer window (`club_goals_relative` worth +0.0054 as a 38-match feature when first added in v1→v2) evaporated once `atkdef` (opponent-adjusted Poisson rating, unbounded horizon) was added — it now measures +0.00018 [−0.00011,+0.00046], meaning the rating absorbed all the horizon signal. — [ml/FINDINGS_WINDOW.md]
- Separately, a train/serve *scope* mismatch was found and fixed: training used a cross-season rolling window (any prior matches regardless of season) while the serving DB query scoped to the current season only, silently causing 88% of August fixtures to get no served prediction and the remainder to see 3–7 match partial windows the model was never trained on distributions for. Ablation: `scoped` (in-season only, matching what was actually served) was a dead null against `xseason` (training recipe) on fixtures both could compute (−0.00001 [−0.00058,+0.00057]) but cost −0.00707 once refusals were priced at the fold base rate (PRICED table, 19,810 fixtures) — "the entire difference between the two live recipes is coverage," not accuracy. The fix shipped: `inference-py/app/db.py` dropped its season_id scoping, aligning serving to the (better) training recipe. — [ml/FINDINGS_SCOPE.md]
- A cheap fix tried in the same phase, `xseason_age` (adding `matches_into_season`/`stale_slots` columns to flag a stale/partial window rather than changing the query), measured +0.00041 [+0.00009,+0.00076] pooled but was *null* specifically in the early-season subset where its stated mechanism should apply (−0.00027 [−0.00139,+0.00082]) — flagged as "significant and probably not real," likely a generic season-progress proxy rather than a genuine window-staleness signal, and not shipped. — [ml/FINDINGS_SCOPE.md]
- Explicitly noted and *still open*: FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md's drift table shows all 5 leagues' home-win rates moving together across multi-year eras by more than they differ from each other cross-sectionally, and "nothing in the model is allowed to know what year it is (on purpose)" — the open item states plainly: "Whether a *recency weighting* on training rows helps is untested and is the question this phase actually turned up." — [ml/FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md, open item 4]
- Related open item from the venue phase: the mild HFA-tracking arm (`hfa_lr10`, a moderate learning-rate increase) beat both a faster-tracking learning rate (`hfa_lr100`) and a 380-fixture rolling window (`hfa_roll`) by +0.00052, described as "the same lesson as FINDINGS_WINDOW.md's flat-bottomed curve: the best estimator of a moving quantity is not the one that moves fastest" — i.e., there is already internal evidence that *some* decay/smoothing constant is a live, sensitive design choice, but it was only tuned for the HFA scalar, not for the form window or the goal-rating learning rate broadly. — [ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md]

### Inferences
- The project has tested window *length* (hard trailing-N cutoff) exhaustively but has never tested window *shape* — i.e., a smooth decay weighting (exponential, or a soft kernel) applied within a window, as opposed to a step function that includes or excludes a match entirely. Given the flat-bottomed curve between 6–24, and given that "the mild [HFA] arm is the one that separates [drift from noise]" was the finding for the analogous HFA case, an exponential-decay form-window is a partially-explored idea worth revisiting: it directly targets the tradeoff the project already identified (a fast-moving estimator overfits noise; a slow one lags true drift) but was only tested for one scalar (HFA), not for the 27-field form vector or for the goal rating's own learning rate.
- Similarly, recency weighting of *training rows* (down-weighting old fixtures in the loss function, distinct from a feature engineering change) is explicitly flagged as untested by the project's own open items and is a clean, well-scoped idea to test next — it addresses the multi-league joint drift finding directly.
- The scope/coverage finding is a caution for any new contextual feature: gating on data availability (e.g., discarding pre-season or promoted-club history) trades training-set size for input-distribution purity, and the project found this trade to be a clear loser (`scoped_full8` lost −0.00153 even on the intersection of fixtures both recipes could serve, purely from losing 15% of training rows) — any new feature should avoid introducing a similar hard gate.

### Gaps
- No test of exponential/soft time-decay within the form window (only hard-cutoff window length was swept).
- No test of recency-weighted training-row loss (down-weighting old seasons), despite being explicitly named as the open question this project's own league-drift analysis surfaced.
- Whether a promoted club's carried-over prior-division form (now proven to span divisions per FINDINGS_SCOPE.md open item 1, since cross-season windows aren't season-gated) is *worth* anything distinct from same-division form is untested and explicitly flagged as open by the project itself.

## External, not-yet-tried techniques: mechanism, evidence, and cost/impact assessment

### Takeaway
The literature strongly supports crowd-driven home advantage and referee bias, schedule-congestion fatigue effects, and altitude/travel effects as real, replicated phenomena — but several of these mechanisms (crowd size, altitude, travel distance) are versions of the *club-level venue-strength* hypothesis this project has already killed twice with strong internal evidence, so their expected marginal value is low unless they can be shown to explain something other than a stable club trait (e.g., a time-varying congestion state or a per-fixture altitude delta, not a club-level average). Two categories look genuinely novel to this project's existing null results: schedule-congestion/rest-days (a per-fixture, time-varying state rather than a club-level average) and cross-league joint rating linking (a different variable entirely from "league identity as a feature," which was tested and killed). Referee-identity and regime-change (managerial-change) detection are well-evidenced in the literature but face serious data-availability and confounding hurdles for this project's implementation.

### Cited Findings

**Home advantage decline / crowd effects (already partially confirmed internally, but with a mechanism this project hasn't isolated)**
- A study of >4,800 games across 11 countries (EPL, Bundesliga, La Liga, Serie A, etc.) during COVID found home advantage roughly halved with empty stadiums: home teams saw 0.7 fewer corners, 1.3 fewer shot attempts, and 0.4 fewer shots on target per game, while away-team output barely changed (+0.10 corners, +0.17 shots, +0.20 shots on target) — the effect is concentrated in home-side underperformance, not away-side improvement. — [University of Leeds](https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news-science/news/article/4894/how-empty-stadiums-affected-football-during-pandemic)
- The same research attributes a meaningful share of the empty-stadium home-advantage loss specifically to *referee behavior*: team dominance (not crowd presence per se) drove referee decisions more, and referees were more severe toward home teams and more lenient toward away teams without a crowd. — [University of Leeds](https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news-science/news/article/4894/how-empty-stadiums-affected-football-during-pandemic)
- A systematic review (PLOS One) of crowd effects on home advantage during COVID and a Chinese Super League study both confirm crowd size (not just crowd presence/absence) is associated with home-advantage magnitude in a dose-response way. — [PLOS One systematic review](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0289899); [Chinese Super League crowd-size study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667239124000261)
- Referee bias is causally linked to crowd presence: "ghost match" (no-crowd) natural experiments during COVID-19 found referees favor the home team specifically when there is an audience, and a full covid-restricted season confirmed the same. Spanish La Liga data show referees added significantly more stoppage time when the home team trailed by one goal, and this effect grew stronger with larger attendance; away teams received more yellow cards as attendance grew. — [ScienceDirect: ghost matches](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176520303815); [PMC: full covid-restricted season](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9261922/); [ScienceDaily: officiating bias](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/04/070403112044.htm)

- **Mechanism vs. this project's null:** These are league-wide, time-varying (season/era) effects, not club-level traits — which matches, not contradicts, `FINDINGS_VENUE.md`'s finding that league-wide HFA moved (0.30→0.20 log-scale) while club-level splits are noise. A crowd-size or attendance-as-proxy feature would need to be a *per-fixture* (this specific match's attendance/capacity ratio) or *era-level* signal, not a club-average, to have any chance of adding beyond what `hfa_lr10`-style era tracking already captures.
- **Risk/caveat:** Attendance data is a market/attendance-derived signal that may be partially endogenous to expected match importance/quality (big games sell out, drawing crowds correlates with team quality) — a confound this project would need to rule out, similar to the `interact`/`placebo` lesson (a plausible-looking signal can be a depth/capacity artifact). Also, per the constraints of this task, if attendance is being used as a *market-derived* signal it would arguably cross into the excluded "market-derived features" category — should be scoped carefully as an intrinsic-schedule/era feature only.
- **Cost:** Low-to-moderate — requires per-fixture attendance data (often available from odds/fixture APIs) and no new rating machinery; could be tested cheaply as a diagnostic (does attendance/capacity ratio correlate with the model's home-win residual, controlling for era) before any feature build, following this project's own "cheap bound first" discipline.
- **Expected impact:** Likely small and largely redundant with the already-tested (and already-adopted-in-spirit) HFA-drift correction; the project's own away-win-residual finding suggests this category is not where the remaining gap lives.

**Schedule congestion / rest days / fixture-fatigue effects (genuinely untested by this project)**
- A Bundesliga-focused study found that under match congestion, a team's *offensive* strength is negatively affected while *defensive* strength improves specifically in home games — a directional, asymmetric effect distinct from a simple "tired team plays worse" story. — [Stüttgen 2025, Journal of Sports Economics via Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15270025251369423)
- Fixture congestion (recovery periods <5 days between matches) is linked to significantly higher injury incidence in professional soccer, via a systematic review; injury and fatigue effects are well-documented mechanisms for degraded on-pitch performance under congestion (though this literature is mostly about injury risk, not directly match outcome probabilities). — [PMC systematic review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9758680/)
- Basketball evidence (NBA) shows scoring is significantly greater in matches following 1–2 rest days versus back-to-back games with none — an analogous congestion effect in a different sport, included as corroborating (not directly transferable) evidence. — [PMC: NBA match congestion](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7925613/)
- **Mechanism:** Days-since-last-match (and secondary fixtures such as midweek European or cup matches) is a per-fixture, time-varying state that neither `venue_lib.py` nor `form_lib.py` currently encodes at all — this is categorically different from anything tested in FINDINGS_VENUE/OPPONENT/LEAGUE/WINDOW, which are all about club identity or long-run averages, not short-term schedule state.
- **Risk/caveat:** This project's leagues appear to be single-competition domestic archives (5 `league_id`s); if the archive does not also capture cup/continental fixtures for the same clubs, "days since last match" will only capture domestic-to-domestic gaps (typically a fixed 3–4 or 7 days), giving little variance to work with — the feature's value depends heavily on whether European/cup fixture dates are joinable into the rest-day calculation. Also risk of the same self-correcting-schedule problem found in FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md: congestion may average out over a form window in the same way opponent strength did, so this should be tested as a *per-fixture* feature (rest days before *this* match) rather than a rolling average.
- **Cost:** Low if only domestic fixture dates are used (just a date-diff calculation per club per fixture); moderate-to-high if it requires ingesting cup/continental schedule data not currently in the pipeline.
- **Expected impact:** Plausible but unproven for *this* dataset — the Bundesliga finding is directional and modest (attack down, defence up in home congested games), likely a few tenths of a percent at best, and untested against this project's own placebo discipline. Should be diagnosed cheaply (variance/reliability check per the project's own rule) before any ablation.

**Travel distance / altitude effects**
- Traveling to higher altitude has a documented negative, linear effect on the away team's physical output (distance covered, high-intensity running down ~3.1% at 1200–1753m vs sea level), while traveling to lower altitude shows no equivalent effect; goals scored and technical/goalkeeping errors did not differ significantly with altitude in at least one study. — [Stanford PDF: Williams, effects of altitude](http://large.stanford.edu/courses/2016/ph241/goldberg1/docs/williams.pdf); [ScienceDirect systematic review, weather/altitude](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772696722000023)
- A key confound noted directly in the literature: teams traveling to high-altitude venues typically also travel *farther*, making altitude and travel-distance effects difficult to separate empirically. — [ResearchGate: altitude and travel distance confound](https://researchgate.net/publication/5693453_Understanding_the_effect_of_altitude_on_physiological_performance_using_results_of_international_football_games)
- **Mechanism vs. this project's null:** Altitude/travel-distance-as-a-club-trait ("this club travels badly") is precisely the hypothesis `FINDINGS_VENUE.md` killed with split-half reliability (r=−0.03) — but altitude/travel-distance is a *venue* (destination) property, not a traveling-club property, so in principle it's a different, testable claim: does playing at *this specific* away ground (higher altitude/farther from the away side's base) shift outcomes, regardless of which two clubs are playing? This project's domestic-league scope (apparently England/other major leagues, given league_ids and typical scale) likely has minimal within-country altitude variation, making this technique low-relevance unless one of the 5 leagues spans meaningfully varying elevations (e.g., a South American or high-altitude-inclusive league).
- **Risk/caveat:** Without knowing the specific leagues behind `league_id` 8/82/301/384/564, this cannot be confirmed as applicable; if all 5 are low-altitude-variance European leagues, this technique has near-zero expected value. Flagged as a **gap requiring the project to check its own league identities** before pursuing.
- **Cost:** Low if venue geocode + elevation lookup is available; the travel-distance calculation itself is trivial (haversine distance between clubs' home grounds).
- **Expected impact:** Very likely negligible for a domestic single-country-per-league dataset with low elevation variance; higher potential only if one league has genuine altitude spread (e.g., includes high-elevation home grounds).

**Referee identity / strictness**
- Referee bias contributing to home advantage is well-replicated (English Premiership analysis, ghost-match natural experiments, and a full covid-restricted season all confirm it), and referees vary in how susceptible they are to crowd influence — "more experienced referees are less biased." — [Full article: Referee bias and home advantage, English Premiership](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640410601038576); [ScienceDirect: home-bias in referee decisions during ghost matches](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176520303815)
- **Mechanism:** A referee-identity feature (per-referee historical home-bias rate, e.g. excess cards/stoppage-time toward home vs away) is genuinely orthogonal to everything tested so far — no venue, opponent, league, or window phase in this project touches match officials.
- **Risk/caveat:** Requires referee-assignment data to be present in the archive at all (not confirmed — none of the read findings docs or code mention a referee field); even where present, referee-level effects are typically small relative to team-strength signal, and a per-referee rating needs enough matches per referee to avoid the same reliability trap FINDINGS_VENUE.md found for club-level splits (a referee officiating <20 matches in the archive would likely show the same "spread is smaller than sampling noise" failure mode). This is exactly the kind of hypothesis the project's own diagnostic discipline (cheap variance/reliability check before any ablation) is built to kill quickly if it's not real.
- **Cost:** Contingent entirely on data availability — if referee ID isn't already ingested, this requires a new data source, which is a nontrivial cost addition, likely higher than any other idea in this report.
- **Expected impact:** Plausibly real per the literature, but likely small in magnitude and only actionable if referee-assignment data is already (or cheaply) available; should be deprioritized until data availability is confirmed.

**Weather / pitch conditions**
- No results were surfaced by this research pass beyond altitude-adjacent studies; a systematic review of weather + altitude effects on physical/technical performance exists but was not fetched in full given time constraints. — [ScienceDirect systematic review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772696722000023) (gap: not fully read)
- **Gap:** Weather/pitch-condition effects on match *outcome probabilities* (as opposed to physical running-performance metrics) were not directly evidenced in this research pass; this is a genuine research gap, not a negative finding — noted honestly per instructions rather than inferred.

**Neutral-venue handling**
- No literature specific to neutral-venue statistical adjustment was retrieved in this pass (not searched directly, given the project's leagues all appear to be regular domestic competitions with no obvious mention of neutral fixtures).
- **Gap:** Whether this project's archive contains any neutral-venue fixtures (playoffs, tournament finals, COVID-relocated matches) at all is unconfirmed — the 5 `league_id`s referenced in FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md read as ordinary domestic top-flight leagues, so this is likely not applicable, but this is inferred, not confirmed by direct code inspection of fixture venue fields.

**Promotion/relegation cross-league strength linking & multi-league joint rating systems**
- Club Elo (clubelo.com) explicitly builds ratings across leagues/countries/continents on one comparable scale, over 3.3M+ men's matches since 1990, using home-field-advantage and inter-league adjustment terms, converging via a daily comparison of home vs away points won per country. — [clubelo.com/System](http://clubelo.com/System)
- A 2026 academic paper proposes a "league-aware club rating framework" using a modified Glicko model with league-aware rating initialization and transition dynamics specifically to model promotion/relegation transitions and enable stable cross-league comparison. — [Springer: league-strength-aware Glicko](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-27272-0_4)
- General approach noted in search synthesis: because clubs never play across country boundaries in normal league play, cross-league links typically require either (a) international/continental competition results (e.g., Champions League/Europa League ties) as a bridge, or (b) explicit promotion/relegation transition modeling (since a promoted club carries information about the level below).
- **Mechanism vs. this project's null:** This is a fundamentally different technique from what `FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md` tested. That phase tested "does the model benefit from knowing *which* league a fixture is in" (identity, dummies, per-league models) — and found no, because the pooled model's base rates already track drift well and leagues aren't meaningfully different in level. Cross-league *rating* linking is a different claim: it says a club *promoted* from League 384's second division carries meaningful prior-division strength information (a version of exactly the open item this project itself flags in FINDINGS_SCOPE.md #1: "nothing has asked whether a promoted side's carried-over form is worth anything"). If the 5 `league_id`s in this archive include divisions of the same footballing pyramid (e.g., a top flight and its second tier), a joint rating scale with promotion/relegation continuity is untested and directly targets an open item this project has already surfaced on its own.
- **Risk/caveat:** Requires knowing whether the 5 leagues in this archive are actually tiers of the same pyramid (in which case this is highly relevant) or 5 unrelated top-flight leagues in different countries (in which case cross-league linking requires international fixtures as a bridge, which this domestic-only archive likely lacks) — this is a **critical unresolved fact** that determines whether this idea is cheap-and-relevant or expensive-and-irrelevant. Flagged as a gap.
- **Cost:** If the 5 leagues are tiers of one pyramid: low-to-moderate (the `AttackDefence` rating already has per-league `mu`/`hfa`/season-boundary regression machinery; extending it to carry a promoted club's rating across the tier boundary instead of resetting it is an incremental, not architectural, change). If leagues are unrelated countries: high (requires a new data source of international fixtures to bridge scales, or abandoning true cross-league comparability).
- **Expected impact:** Directly relevant to a real open item this project flagged itself (promoted-club carried-over form); could plausibly help the specific subset of fixtures involving recently promoted/relegated clubs, though this is a narrow slice of any season (~3–4 clubs of ~20 per league per year) so the aggregate log-loss impact is likely small even if the effect per affected fixture is real.

**Regime-change (managerial change) detection as a decay-reset trigger**
- Evidence on managerial-change effects is genuinely mixed: about 68% of new managers show a short-term "bounce" (avg +0.68 points/game over first 5 matches), but only 41% sustain it through matches 11–20, and using entropy-balancing (a more rigorous causal-inference method) some studies find "no consistent gains to performance following either a dismissal or a quit" compared to a counterfactual of no change. — [Football Perspectives / summarized research on EPL managerial change](https://footballperspectives.org/impact-managerial-change-english-premier-league/); [ResearchGate: entropy balancing null result](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222578644_The_impact_of_managerial_change_on_team_performance_in_professional_sports)
- New coaches measurably affect tactical/physical metrics (high-speed running, metabolic load) that show up in underlying performance metrics (e.g., xG) before they show up in results. — [PMC: managerial change physical performance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12298339/); [performanceodds.com: managerial change and xG](https://www.performanceodds.com/how-to-guides/how-managerial-changes-affect-goals-xg-and-results/)
- **Mechanism:** A managerial-change flag would function as a decay-reset trigger — i.e., discount or discard a club's pre-change rating/form history at the point of a manager change, rather than smoothly decaying it. This is conceptually related to this project's still-open recency-weighting question (FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md open item 4) but targets a *discrete event* rather than smooth time decay.
- **Risk/caveat:** The academic evidence is genuinely mixed/contested (rigorous causal designs find null effects on results, even though effects on underlying performance metrics like xG are more consistently found) — this is exactly the kind of "significant in aggregate literature, uncertain whether it clears this project's own null-first bar" hypothesis that the project's own diagnostic discipline would likely kill quickly, similar to how club venue strength and opponent-adjusted form both looked plausible from outside literature but failed the project's own variance/reliability checks. Also requires managerial-change-date data, which is not currently in this project's pipeline (an external data source would be needed).
- **Cost:** Moderate — needs new managerial-tenure data (not in the current archive) plus a decay-reset mechanism in the rating classes (`AttackDefence`/`VenueAttackDefence` already have season-boundary regression logic that could be adapted for an in-season reset).
- **Expected impact:** Uncertain and contested even in the primary literature; given this project's demonstrated pattern of externally-plausible hypotheses failing its own reliability checks (club venue trait, opponent-adjusted form), this should be treated as a low-confidence, moderate-cost idea — worth a cheap diagnostic (does a manager-change flag show any reliable pre/post shift in this archive's own data) before any feature build.

### Inferences
- The single external idea most likely to add value cheaply, given everything this project has already ruled out, is genuine exponential/soft time-decay (on the form window and/or as recency-weighted training-row loss) — not because the external literature is stronger here (recency weighting is a well-known but generic ML/rating-systems technique, not a football-specific finding), but because it directly targets an open item this project's own analysis surfaced (FINDINGS_LEAGUE.md item 4) and because the closest analogous test the project already ran (HFA learning-rate sweep) showed a moderate/mild smoothing constant beating both a static and an aggressively-tracking one — suggesting the same shape of result (a moderate decay constant beating both a flat window and an aggressive one) is plausible for the form window and/or training-row weighting too.
- The second most promising external idea is cross-league/promotion-relegation rating linkage — but its expected value is entirely conditional on a fact this research pass could not confirm: whether the project's 5 `league_id`s include tiers of the same domestic pyramid. This should be the first thing checked before any implementation work, since it's a near-zero-cost check (a database query) that determines whether the idea is even applicable.
- Crowd/attendance, referee identity, altitude/travel, and managerial-change ideas are all well-evidenced in the general football-analytics/econometrics literature, but each maps onto a category of hypothesis (club-level trait, rare/small-sample identity effect, or contested-effect-size event) that this project's own findings have repeatedly shown fails its variance/reliability pre-checks even when a real underlying mechanism exists (as with opponent-adjustment, where the per-match mechanism was confirmed real but the aggregated feature was still worth nothing). Each should be diagnosed cheaply (per this project's own established methodology) before any ablation is built, and none should be assumed to pay off just because the general literature supports the mechanism.
- Schedule congestion/rest-days is the one idea in this list that targets a per-fixture, time-varying state rather than a club-level average or rare identity — making it structurally different from every hypothesis this project has already killed, and therefore the one most worth a first-pass diagnostic even though this research pass found no football-specific outcome-probability evidence (only performance/injury evidence) to quantify its expected impact.

### Gaps
- Whether the archive's 5 league_ids represent unrelated countries or tiers of a shared pyramid (critical fact for cross-league linking's relevance) — not resolved in this research pass; recommend the report writer or project owner check this directly against `ml/league_lib.py` / the fixtures table before prioritizing that idea.
- Whether the archive contains referee-assignment or attendance data at all — not confirmed by reading FINDINGS or the lib files in scope; both external ideas (referee identity, crowd/attendance-as-proxy) are contingent on this and were not verifiable without inspecting the raw data schema, which was out of scope for this research pass.
- Direct academic evidence quantifying schedule-congestion effects specifically on match *outcome probabilities* (as opposed to injury risk or physical/fatigue metrics) in football was not found in this pass — the closest evidence (Bundesliga attack/defence asymmetry under congestion) is directional but the magnitude wasn't quantified in the retrieved source, and injury/fatigue literature doesn't translate directly to win-probability effect sizes.
- Weather/pitch-condition effects on match outcome (not just physical performance) were not directly evidenced; flagged as an open gap rather than assumed negative.
- Neutral-venue prevalence in this project's own archive was not directly confirmed (inferred as likely absent/irrelevant from league_id context, not verified).
