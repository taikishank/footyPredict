# Market blending is the untried lever v4 needs

The single most promising path to a v4 gain is one this project has never actually built: feeding de-vigged market probability into the model as an input rather than using it only as a benchmark. Five research tracks — goal-model architecture, contextual features, market/calibration, player-level features, and training methodology — converge on the same diagnosis. Against a de-vigged Pinnacle closing line, the model (currently logging **0.98922** in its full squad-augmented configuration, against a team-only ablation baseline of 0.98121) trails the market by **0.0211 log loss**, and that gap has been proven to be *missing signal, not miscalibration*: temperature scaling, the one recalibration method tried, made things worse (`ml/FINDINGS_MARKET.md`). The gap concentrates overwhelmingly in two buckets — **61.8% on away wins, 35.5% on draws, only 2.7% on home wins** — and every rating-mechanism fix aimed at those buckets (club-level venue splits, HFA drift correction, opponent-strength adjustment, coupled venue states, residual bounding) has already been tried and killed. That combination — a proven information gap, a diagnosed mechanism (the market discriminates ~25% better than the model on identical fixtures), and a dead end in the rating-mechanism family that used to be the project's main lever — is what makes market-blending the standout candidate rather than one option among several. The rest of this report ranks eight other candidates against it: a cheap, already-open item on refit cadence; a genuinely novel recency-decay experiment; two lineup-data extensions Phase 9 explicitly deferred; and a long tail of externally well-evidenced ideas (hierarchical shrinkage, bivariate Poisson, Glicko-style uncertainty, cross-league rating links) that mostly land in the same "plausible but small, likely redundant" territory the project has repeatedly found for shape-level tweaks.

## Market-blending targets the exact gap three tracks failed to close by other means

The market-and-calibration research and the goal-model-architecture research independently arrive at the same conclusion from different angles, which is itself the strongest evidence in this report: nothing has ever tried using the market as a *feature*. `ml/FINDINGS_MARKET.md` shows the model's probabilities are compressed toward the base rate specifically where the market is most confident (model 0.5872 vs. market 0.6674 vs. actual 0.6881 in the top confidence decile), and that this survives a monotone post-hoc fix — temperature scaling cost −0.00039 log loss on held-out data rather than helping, which rules out every classical recalibration technique (Platt, isotonic, beta, temperature) by the same logic, since they are all monotone transforms of one already-computed score. A logistic/log-odds blend — fitting `logit(p_final) = a + b1·logit(p_model) + b2·logit(p_market)` on held-out folds — is structurally different because it injects a second, independently-discriminating signal rather than reshaping the first one; it is also a strict superset of "ignore the market" (b2=0), so on the fitting data it cannot underperform the status quo, though a genuinely held-out test is still required to trust the result. The literature backs the mechanism even where it can't quantify it for this exact setup: Egidi, Pauli & Torelli's hierarchical Poisson model blends historical rates with bookmaker-implied rates explicitly because "the well-known strong relationship between football models and betting markets" is underused ([arXiv 1802.08848](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08848), though the PDF could not be fully extracted in this research pass), and a separate line of work shows betting-odds-implied probabilities beat goals-based Elo updates outright because odds encode information — team news, lineups, money — that a results-only model structurally cannot see ([PLOS ONE, ELO-Odds](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0198668)). The cost is low: Pinnacle opening and closing prices are already downloaded, joined, and de-vigged in `ml/market_lib.py` and `ml/build_market_index.py`, and two de-vig methods are already implemented and validated. The two real risks are leakage and interpretation. Leakage: the blend must be trained on **opening**, not closing, prices if it is to reflect what a live system actually has at serve time — using closing prices would quietly re-introduce exactly the kind of look-ahead bug the project's own leak-check discipline (commit `1f516d3`) was built to catch. Interpretation: once the market is inside the model, "beating the market" stops being an independent skill measure, and a blend weighted entirely toward the market is mathematically capped at the market's own 0.96293 log loss — it cannot out-forecast the market using only the market's own price. The project's own arithmetic sets the realistic ceiling: closing the entire 0.0211 gap was estimated to need "three or four more findings the size of the ones already banked" (`ml/FINDINGS_MARKET.md`), so a market blend should be read as a partial, not total, capture of that budget — but it is the only candidate in this report that directly attacks a *proven* information deficit rather than a hypothesized one.

## Away-win and draw prediction have already defeated the rating-mechanism toolkit

Three tracks — venue, opponent-adjustment, and the RWI replication study — each independently confirm that the project's usual lever (tweak the rating update rule) is now exhausted for these two buckets, which matters because it redirects where v4 effort should go. Club-level home-field advantage as a stable trait does not exist in the archive: a club's home-minus-away points-per-game gap has a split-half reliability of **r = −0.03**, and the club-to-club spread (SD 0.373 ppg) is smaller than pure sampling noise (SD 0.431 ppg expected from ~19 matches/venue) would produce on its own (`ml/FINDINGS_VENUE.md`). League-wide HFA genuinely fell across the 26-season archive (log-scale 0.30 → 0.20), and correcting the frozen online estimate bought a correctly-signed but statistically null **+0.00035** — the tracking-rate sweep also showed, tellingly, that a moderate learning rate beat both a static one and an aggressive one, foreshadowing a pattern relevant below. Opponent-strength adjustment of the 27 raw form statistics was pre-registered with a strict bar and failed it decisively: none of 27 fields reached the required 5% schedule-variance share, because round-robin scheduling is self-correcting — a club's last 8 opponents are already ~80% as varied as 8 drawn at random, so any opponent-strength signal averages out over the form window (`ml/FINDINGS_OPPONENT.md`). Coupled venue states (a partial-sharing axis between fully independent and fully shared home/away ratings, from the RWI replication) were dead across the entire coupling range, and residual bounding on rating updates only found a shallow, noise-band optimum that actively hurt away-win prediction — the model's single worst outcome — at its aggressive end (`ml/FINDINGS_RWI.md`). The project's own synthesis states this plainly: away-win prediction remains the worst-predicted outcome even after every venue-parameter fix, and "whatever identifies a strong away side is not a venue parameter and not a calibration constant." This is why market-blending (above) and genuinely new per-fixture context — not another rating-shape tweak — are where this report weights its remaining recommendations. Two candidates fit that description and are untried: recency-decay weighting and schedule-congestion state. Recency weighting (an exponential/soft decay applied within the form window, or on training-row loss weights, rather than the current hard trailing-8-match cutoff) is explicitly flagged as open by the project's own league-drift analysis ("nothing in the model is allowed to know what year it is, on purpose") and is analogically supported by the HFA learning-rate result, where a moderate tracking rate beat both extremes — the same shape of result plausibly transfers to the form window's decay constant, though this remains untested for that specific parameter. Schedule congestion (days since last match, a per-fixture time-varying state neither `venue_lib.py` nor `form_lib.py` currently encodes) is structurally different from every hypothesis above because it is not a club-level average or rare identity effect — a Bundesliga study found congestion asymmetrically hurts home offense while improving home defense ([Stüttgen 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15270025251369423)) — but no football-specific evidence quantifies its effect on outcome *probabilities* (only on injury risk and physical output), and its value depends on whether the archive's fixture calendar captures cup/continental dates, which is unconfirmed. Cross-league/promotion-relegation rating linkage is a third genuinely different idea (not the already-killed "league identity as a feature") that directly targets an open item the project flagged itself — whether a promoted club's carried-over prior-division strength is worth anything — but its relevance is entirely gated on a single unverified fact: whether the five `league_id`s in this archive are tiers of one pyramid or five unrelated top flights. That is a near-zero-cost database check that should happen before any other work on this idea.

## Cheap, already-flagged architecture wins sit ahead of any new modeling family

Before reaching for a new statistical framework, the goal-model-architecture and training-methodology tracks surface several open items that are cheaper than they look because the machinery already exists. The largest standing finding in the whole project — pooling the Dixon-Coles score matrix with the XGBoost classifier in log-odds space, rather than feeding one model's output to the other as a column — is worth **+0.00184** at the correctly-tuned depth-2 tree (down from a since-explained +0.00341 at the over-fit depth-4 tree that was also absorbing noise-column placebos as if they were signal; `ml/FINDINGS_CAPACITY.md`). Two follow-ons to that finding are explicitly open and cheap: the refit/staleness cadence that was swept for the two-parameter Dixon-Coles score model (half-life 365 days, refit every 7 days, worth +0.00323 over an online-rate approximation) has **never been measured for the full 63-column v3 classifier itself**, and the blend weight between the two forecasters was observed declining across folds (0.58→0.38), suggesting it should be refit online rather than frozen — both are measurement gaps on infrastructure that already exists, not new builds. A third open item, replacing the fixed `RIDGE = 5.0` shrinkage constant with a data-estimated (empirical-Bayes or hierarchical) precision, targets a design choice the code's own comments already flag as a deliberate simplification; the literature ([Baio & Blangiardo 2010](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/16040/1/16040.pdf); [arXiv 1807.09236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.09236)) argues this is generically better than a fixed constant, though no source gives a quantified log-loss delta for this specific substitution, and a known failure mode (overshrinkage of genuinely elite/weak clubs) is a real risk to check for. Further out on the cost curve, a full bivariate Poisson (a shared third Poisson component correlating all scorelines, versus the Dixon-Coles τ-correction that already only patches the low-score corner) is a strict generalization of what's shipped, but the project's own finding that the τ-correction was worth **+0.00085 standalone and zero once blended** is a strong prior that a fuller correlation term lands in the same small, shape-only regime the project has repeatedly found unproductive — worth a cheap standalone probe, not a priority build. Glicko-2-style rating uncertainty is a more structurally distinct idea worth flagging separately: because attack/defence ratings already strictly dominate every Elo variant tested, a straight port of Glicko-2 as a *forecaster* is unlikely to help, but its uncertainty term — fed to the classifier as a feature distinguishing "few recent matches, wide confidence" from "settled form, narrow confidence" — is not a monotone transform of the point estimate, which is exactly the category of fix the temperature-scaling failure did *not* rule out. This is speculative (no source measures it as an auxiliary XGBoost feature) but cheap to prototype since the rating machinery already exists.

## Player-level and lineup features have two live gaps and one internal contradiction to resolve

Phase 9 already banked the largest recent single-block gain in the project — the availability tier (who's in the XI, how settled, who's missing) cleared its pre-registered bar at **+0.01057 to +0.01204 log loss**, roughly 13% of everything reducible on this dataset — but the mechanism turned out to be squad-quality/experience proxying (via `xi_relative_experience`, worth +0.00536 alone) rather than the "team news" effect that motivated the build; the gain was, if anything, flattest on the most disrupted lineups, directly contradicting the original hypothesis. Three separate attempts to build a better importance-weighting scheme for absent key players (head-count, minutes-weighted, goal-involvement-weighted) all failed within noise of each other, closing that specific avenue: "not a weighting problem." Two genuinely open items follow directly from this. First, a real injury/suspension list distinct from rotation-inferred absence — the project's own probe already confirmed SportMonks' `sidelined` include returns usable injury/suspension category data per fixture, and a `player_sidelined` table exists in schema but is unpopulated by any writer; this is explicitly a data-plumbing task, not a new data-access problem, and directly targets the reason the availability block reads as squad-quality rather than team news. Second, the train/serve gap between predicted (pre-48h, no bench) and confirmed lineups is flagged as unmeasured and is a live production risk: the training archive is entirely confirmed lineups, but serving traffic inside 48 hours of kickoff is entirely predicted ones, and this has never been quantified. Externally, market-value-weighted lineup strength (Transfermarkt-style) is the best-evidenced untried idea in this space — Peeters (2018) found it beats FIFA-ranking-based team strength ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207017300754)), and Arntzen & Hvattum (2021) found team ratings and player ratings are *complementary* covariates, each adding value beyond the other ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1471082X20929881)) — which is worth noting sits in some tension with Phase 9's own finding that its home-grown performance/ratings tier was redundant with team form; the discrepancy is plausibly that market value is a cleaner, longer-horizon "how good is this player" signal than a per-90-rate rating computed only from 2016 onward, but this project has no internal evidence either way. One recommendation from the player-level notes needs a caveat before it is acted on: Phase 9 flags extending the team-form window to 38 matches (per the accidental `club_goals_relative` discovery) as the "cheapest, most promising" next experiment — but the contextual-features track's own exhaustive window sweep (4 to 38 matches, `ml/FINDINGS_WINDOW.md`) already tested exactly this and found the curve flat-bottomed between 6 and 24 matches, with the best 38-match arm's confidence interval touching zero and explicitly not promoted. These two notes appear to disagree, and the likely resolution is that `club_goals_relative` measured something the *statistics* window doesn't capture (a goals-only, opponent-agnostic scoring rate) that a later `atkdef` rating with an unbounded horizon had already absorbed by the time the general window sweep ran — but this should be confirmed, not assumed, before either recommendation is acted on for v4.

## Conclusion

The clearest lesson across all five research tracks is not any single feature idea but a shift in what kind of idea is left to try. Every hypothesis shaped like "adjust the rating update rule" — venue splits, opponent adjustment, coupled states, residual bounding, league identity — has now been tested and killed, often more than once, and the project's own placebo and seed-band discipline (SD 0.00006–0.00007 from reseeding alone, with 11% of same-recipe pairs clearing significance by chance) means several of the smaller candidates in this report deserve real skepticism even if they post a positive number. What remains genuinely open falls into three different shapes: information the model has never been given access to (the market's price, real injury data, market-value lineup strength), timing/state the model has never modeled (recency decay, schedule congestion, predicted-vs-confirmed lineup drift), and measurement gaps on machinery that already exists (v3's own refit cadence, online blend-weight refitting). Market-blending is the standout because it is the only candidate in this list backed by a *proven* deficit rather than a plausible mechanism — the model's under-discrimination relative to the market has already survived one attempted debunking (temperature scaling) that would have explained it away as a calibration artifact. The project's own arithmetic is the honest final word: reaching full market parity was estimated to need three or four findings the size of the ones already banked, and nothing in this report — market blend included — should be sold as a single fix for the 0.0211 gap. It should be sold as the next-highest-expected-value item on a list the project has already proven it knows how to prune ruthlessly.
